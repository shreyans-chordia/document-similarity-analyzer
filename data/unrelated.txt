The monsoon season in India usually begins in June and lasts until September. Farmers depend heavily on these rains to water their crops, especially rice, cotton and sugarcane. A good monsoon can boost the rural economy, while a weak one can lead to drought and rising food prices. Meteorologists study ocean temperatures and wind patterns to predict how strong the rains will be each year. Cities also face problems during heavy rainfall, such as flooding, traffic jams and damaged roads.
